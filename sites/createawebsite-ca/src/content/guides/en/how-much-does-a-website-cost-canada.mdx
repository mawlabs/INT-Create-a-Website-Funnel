## What moves the price

A website quote is mostly hours. Every line below adds or removes hours.

### How many pages, and who plans them

A five-page site (home, about, services, contact, one more) is the baseline. Every extra page type is a new design and a new build. Ten similar pages cost less than four very different ones, because the second, third and fourth "services" pages reuse the first. Sitemaps are cheap to draw and expensive to change after the design stage, so settle the page list first.

### Custom design or a theme

A purchased theme, styled to your brand, is the cheapest way to look presentable. It is also the reason so many small-business sites look alike. A custom design from components (our basic tier, <Price of="price:websites.basic" />) gives you a layout that is yours without paying for every screen from scratch. A fully custom build (<Price of="price:websites.custom" />) starts with a discovery phase and is for sites where the layout itself is part of the product.

### Selling online

The moment you sell something, the site needs product pages, a cart, checkout, payment, shipping rules, taxes for every province you ship to, order emails and a way to manage inventory. That is why a store starts at <Price of="min:shopify.basic" /> even with a purchased theme, and why "just add a shop" to an existing site is rarely small. Shopify handles more of that out of the box; WooCommerce gives you more control and more to maintain.

### Integrations

Booking calendars, CRM forms, newsletters, accounting, membership, a warehouse system. Each one is a few hours if the platform has a good plugin, and days if it doesn't. List everything the site has to talk to before you ask for a quote. It is the question that changes the number most, and the one people forget.

### Two languages

In Quebec, a business site has to be in French, at least as prominently as any other language. That is the Charter of the French Language, updated by Bill 96, not a preference. Doing it properly means a second set of every page, every button label and every email, plus a language switch that lands on the same page in the other language. Budget roughly 15 to 30 percent on top of a unilingual site, more if the content is long. Machine translation is cheaper and reads like it.

### Who writes the content

Content is the most common cause of late launches. If you write it, the site is cheaper and slower. If we write it, add hours for interviews, drafts and revisions. Either way, decide before the design stage, because layouts are designed around real text lengths.

### Photos and brand

A logo, a colour palette and a handful of good photos are inputs, not outputs. If you don't have them, they are a separate line item or a separate supplier.

## DIY builder, freelancer or studio

There are three ways to get a website. Each is the right answer for someone.

| | DIY builder | Freelancer | Studio |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cost | $20–$60 a month, plus your evenings | $1,000–$4,000 typical | <Price of="price:websites.basic..websites.custom" /> |
| Time to first version | A weekend to a few weeks | 3–8 weeks | 4–10 weeks |
| Who does the work | You | One person | A team: design, development, content, QA |
| Best when | A simple site, a tight budget, and you like tinkering | A small site with a clear brief | Bilingual, e-commerce, custom features, or a site your revenue depends on |

A DIY builder such as Wix, Squarespace or a Shopify theme is the honest choice for a one-page site, a hobby, a side project, or a business that needs a presence and nothing more. Templates look like templates, French is on you, and the subscription adds up over the years, but it works.

A freelancer is one person, which is the strength and the risk. A good one will build a small site with a clear brief for less than a studio. Ask who fixes it in a year, and what happens if they are booked when you need them.

A studio is a team: design, development, content and quality assurance, with someone accountable for the whole thing. It is not the cheapest option. It is the right one when the site has to earn its keep, when it has to work in two languages, or when it has to connect to something.

## The costs people forget

The build is the biggest number, but not the only one.

- **Domain name.** About $20 a year for a `.ca`. Keep it in your own name, always.
- **Hosting.** From a few dollars a month for a small WordPress site to $50 or more for a busy store. Shopify's plan includes hosting.
- **Maintenance.** Updates, backups, security patches, small changes. Our plans run <Price of="price:maintenance.light..maintenance.premium" /> a month at <Price of="maintenanceRate" /> an hour, or you can do it yourself after a hand-over.
- **Apps and plugins.** Booking, reviews, advanced forms, email marketing. Many are free; the useful ones are often $10 to $50 a month each.
- **Translation.** If the French is written by a person, budget for it.
- **Photography.** Stock photos are the reason sites look like stock photos. A half-day with a local photographer is a better investment than most people expect.
- **Your time.** Content, feedback rounds, approvals. A site that waits three weeks for a photo launches three weeks late.
